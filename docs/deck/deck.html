The bottleneck

Criteria are carried forward from precedent, without a documented rationale

  • Lab, ECOG and washout limits are often copied from the last protocol. Nobody writes down why each one is there, so nobody can tell which ones still have a reason.
  • 57% of protocols have at least one substantial amendment; 45% of those amendments were judged avoidable (Getz et al. 2016).
  • Protocol teams rarely get evidence-based feedback on a draft until it is too late to change cheaply (Regeneron, Trial by Design Pt 2).
  • FDA final guidance (July 2026) covers exactly these criteria: lab values, performance status, washout periods. For older trials we apply it retrospectively and label it so.
"Unnecessarily restrictive eligibility criteria may slow subject accrual, limit patients' access to clinical trials, and lead to trial results that do not fully represent treatment effects…"
FDA final guidance, Cancer Clinical Trial Eligibility Criteria: Laboratory Values (July 2026), §I

But "loosen the criteria" misses the point

Restrictions have reasons: patient safety with myelosuppressive chemotherapy, enrichment, interpretable adverse events. The question is whether each limit's rationale is documented and still holds.

Two entry modes

One engine, two points in the protocol's life

A · Finishing a trial

Review an existing protocol before it is finalized or amended
  • Reads the posted protocol PDF: each criterion with its verbatim line and page
  • Rationale badge per criterion, with the arm whose drugs drive it
  • Compare with earlier comparable trials; Ask; Redraft with live checks
  • Decide with structured reasons; export a review packet

B · Create criteria from an idea

Draft criteria for a new concept: condition, drugs, setting, phase
  • Finds precedent trials that started before the design date
  • Drafts each criterion from a verified precedent value or FDA passage
  • No verified source: Insufficient evidence, never a guess
  • Self-critique of every draft; before/after against a real protocol

Mode A · Finishing a trial

Each criterion gets a rationale, a comparison and a checked redraft

Rationale badges

Review recommended Rationale supports keeping Insufficient evidence

Likely purpose (safety, efficacy enrichment, regulatory precedent), inferred unless the protocol states it, and an arm line: which arm's drugs are linked to the toxicity.

Compare and Ask

Side by side with comparable trials that started earlier, with regimen coverage and page links. Ask answers questions from the provenance graph; every statement is checked against it.

Redraft workspace

Type another limit and the deterministic checks run live: precedent, FDA passage, regimen logic. Outcomes: Supported · Qualified · Rejected · Needs medical review.

Verify

Challenge any flag. A plausible argument with a true premise can still be rejected when another arm contradicts it.

Decide

Structured reasons: safety concern, regulatory requirement, efficacy enrichment, prior evidence, operational reason; free text only for exceptions.

Review packet

Verbatim requirement and page, rationale for and against, open questions, next action and the team's decision. Markdown and JSON.

Mode A on a real protocol · EMPOWER-Lung 3 (NCT03409614)

Hemoglobin ≥10 g/dL: a review question, not a mistake

  • Own rationale first: the arms include platinum doublets with pemetrexed or paclitaxel; the protocol notes higher grade 3–4 anemia with pemetrexed
  • A safety rationale for a threshold exists; the precise value is not established by the available evidence
  • Earlier peers: CheckMate 9LA, CheckMate 227 and KEYNOTE-042 used ≥9 g/dL; eNERGY used >10 g/dL → Review recommended
  • Verify: "cemiplimab is not myelosuppressive, so lower the ANC floor" is Rejected: other arms contain carboplatin, cisplatin, paclitaxel, pemetrexed
  • Not claimed: that 10 is wrong, or that a lower limit is safe
A more permissive comparator shows a threshold was used, not that it was safe · posted protocol is Amendment 55
Mode B · Create criteria from an idea

Verifier-gated drafts, then a before/after against EMPOWER-Lung 3 as written

Idea: first-line metastatic NSCLC; cemiplimab, ipilimumab, platinum doublet with pemetrexed or paclitaxel; phase 3; design date March 2018.

  • Precedents limited to trials that started before the design date
  • A value is drafted only when the verifier confirms it in a precedent PDF or an FDA passage; otherwise Insufficient evidence
  • Self-critique: each draft is reviewed by the same engine that reviews finished protocols
  • Differences from the real protocol are review questions, not errors

Agent risks and mitigations

The deterministic verifier is the authority, not the model

RiskMitigation
A model invents a value, a quote or a trialEvery statement is checked against the provenance graph; numbers, quotes and trial IDs must match. The model may only downgrade a flag, never upgrade it
A drafted criterion has no real basisDrafts need a verified precedent or FDA passage; otherwise Insufficient evidence
Agreement mistaken for evidenceAgreement among comparators may be template carry-forward; a looser peer shows use, not safety
Instructions hidden in a PDFDocument text is data, never instructions; injected-instruction defects are in the eval
Causal or outcome overclaimsNo claims of safety, enrollment or cost gains, or causality between trials; wording checks in tests
Patient privacyPublic data only: ClinicalTrials.gov, posted protocols, FDA guidance, NHANES. No patient data
Blind spotRationale in non-public sources (preclinical toxicology, internal development data, regulator correspondence) cannot be seen by ProtocolOS
Not clinically validated; outputs are review questions for the protocol team9

Scope and what's next

What ProtocolOS is, and isn't

Is

  • A working, deployed rationale reviewer and drafter for lab, ECOG and washout criteria in oncology
  • Grounded in public protocols and FDA final guidance (July 2026), with a deterministic verifier
  • MIT licensed, documented install, reproducible evals

Isn't (yet)

  • Clinically validated: rule-based, single-annotator gold, no clinician review
  • A recruitment, cost or safety predictor; we make no such claims
  • Next: internal precedent and amendments · ICH M11 structured protocols · clinician review
