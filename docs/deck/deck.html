The bottleneck

Eligibility criteria are written from precedent, and reviewed too late

  • Protocol teams "rarely get evidence-based feedback on their draft until it's too late to change cheaply" (Regeneron, Trial by Design Pt 2).
  • 57% of protocols have ≥1 substantial amendment; 45% of those judged avoidable (Getz et al. 2016).
  • FDA finalized three oncology guidances in July 2026 on exactly the criteria most often templated: lab values, performance status, washout periods. For older trials we apply them retrospectively, as today's standard.
"Unnecessarily restrictive eligibility criteria may slow subject accrual, limit patients' access to clinical trials, and lead to trial results that do not fully represent treatment effects…"
FDA, Cancer Clinical Trial Eligibility Criteria: Laboratory Values (July 2026), §I, lines 37–39

Why it's hard

"Loosen the criteria" misses most of the problem

Restrictions have reasons

  • Patient safety (e.g. myelosuppressive chemotherapy)
  • Predictive and prognostic enrichment
  • Reduced heterogeneity → statistical power
  • Interpretability of adverse events

FDA Enrichment Strategies (2019); Regeneron mentor insights §1.4–1.5

The evidence is scattered

EMPOWER-Lung 3 (Regeneron, NCT03409614): the registry text never mentions the neutrophil threshold. The lab criteria are on page 67 of a 170-page PDF. Comparable trials' thresholds sit in other sponsors' PDFs.

What it does

From protocol PDF to criterion-level evidence

Read

Extracts lab, ECOG and washout criteria with the verbatim line and page. Handles tables, Symbol-font glyphs, image-only pages.

Compare

Finds comparable trials (same disease, setting, histology, drug classes, started earlier), parses their PDFs, checks regimen coverage.

Ground

32 FDA passages (2026 eligibility guidances + 2019 enrichment), verified verbatim with section, page and line.

Classify

Review recommended Evidence supports retaining Insufficient evidence plus an inferred purpose and missing evidence.

Verify

Factual checks + clinical-logic rules. Outcomes: Supported · Qualified · Rejected · Needs medical review.

Decide

Exportable review packet with decision fields (Markdown / PDF / JSON).
Deterministic, auditable engine; optional model may only downgrade and is always verified5
Real example · EMPOWER-Lung 3

Hemoglobin ≥10 g/dL: a review question, not a mistake

  • Own rationale first: arms include platinum doublets with pemetrexed or paclitaxel; protocol p.51 notes higher grade 3–4 anemia with pemetrexed
  • A plausible safety rationale exists, but the precise threshold is not established by the available evidence
  • Peers (started earlier): CheckMate 9LA, CheckMate 227 and KEYNOTE-042 used ≥9 g/dL; eNERGY used >10 g/dL
  • A looser peer threshold is precedent, not proof of safety → Review recommended
  • Not claimed: that 10 is wrong, that it changed across amendments, or that EMPOWER-Lung 1 shaped it
Posted protocol is Amendment 5, not necessarily the design-time version · all values link to protocol pages6
Challengeable

The verifier rejects a plausible argument with a true premise

Challenge (typed example): "Cemiplimab is a PD-1 inhibitor and not myelosuppressive, so lower the ANC floor to 1.0."

  • Premise checks pass (factual)
  • regimen_contradiction fails (clinical-logic): other arms contain carboplatin, cisplatin, paclitaxel, pemetrexed → Rejected
  • A regimen-matched relaxation (Hgb 9.0) is not approved either: it becomes Needs medical review

We label which checks are factual and which are hand-written rules. Neither is clinical validation.

Honest scope and what's next

What TrialLens is, and isn't

Is

  • A working, reproducible reviewer for lab, ECOG and washout criteria in oncology
  • Complementary to criteria-drafting tools (e.g. Regeneron/Keiji ISPOR 2026): they draft from precedent; we challenge individual restrictions
  • MIT-licensed, runs from a clean clone, 13 tests

Isn't (yet)

  • Clinically validated: rule-based, single-annotator gold, no clinician review
  • A recruitment, cost or safety predictor; we make no such claims
  • Next: internal precedent and amendments · learning from team decisions · ICH M11 / USDM structured protocols · clinician review
